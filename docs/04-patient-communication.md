# 04 – Patient communication & boundaries

The technology only works if expectations are set. Patients who know the rules follow them. Patients who don't know the rules text you at midnight.

## Set expectations in writing

Give every patient who gets your number a short written policy. Put it in the intake packet and the portal, and send it as the first message from the patient line. Start from the [patient communication policy template](../templates/patient-communication-policy.md). It covers:

- **What the number is for.** Non-urgent questions, scheduling, follow-up.
- **What it is NOT for.** Emergencies (call 911), new or worsening chest pain, stroke symptoms, suicidal thoughts (988).
- **Response times.** For example, "within 1 business day" or "same day during office hours."
- **Hours.** When messages are read, and what happens after hours.
- **Texting risks and consent.** SMS isn't fully secure. The patient agrees to text anyway, or chooses secure messaging or the portal.
- **Documentation.** Messages may become part of the medical record.
- **Who else may read it.** Staff or covering clinicians.

Record consent to text or email in the chart.

## Configure the patient line

| Control | Setting |
|---|---|
| **Office hours** | Match your real availability. Outside hours, calls go to voicemail or the answering service, and texts get an auto-reply. |
| **After-hours auto-reply** | Emergency instructions plus the expected response time. See [templates](../templates/auto-replies.md). |
| **Voicemail greeting** | Clinic name, no personal name if you prefer, emergency instructions, expected callback time. See [templates](../templates/voicemail-greetings.md). |
| **After-hours routing** | Answering service or on-call colleague for true urgent issues, if your practice model supports it. |
| **Team inbox** | Add your MA/nurse/front desk so the line doesn't depend on you alone. |
| **Vacation mode** | A covering clinician and an updated auto-reply. |
| **Caller ID for outbound** | The patient-facing number itself. You reach patients from the same number they reach you on. |

## Device-level boundaries (Android "Modes" / Do Not Disturb)

Recent Android versions have **Modes** (Settings → Modes, formerly Do Not Disturb schedules):

- **"Clinic hours" mode:** everything allowed.
- **"Off duty" mode:** the patient-line app's notifications are silenced. Personal starred contacts ring through. **Repeat callers** can break through if you want an escape hatch.
- **"On call" mode:** the patient-line / on-call app is allowed to ring through while other work apps stay silent.
- **"Sleep" mode:** only starred personal contacts and the on-call app, if you're actually on call.

Tips:
- Notification channels are per app. In the patient-line app, allow the "urgent" or "calls" channel and silence "messages" after hours.
- Because notifications forward from the Clinical profile to Owner, check that DND and Modes apply the way you expect **while you're in the Owner profile.** Test it.
- If you're *not* on call, you can **End session** on the Clinical profile at night and it stops completely (see [08](08-daily-operations.md)). The answering service or auto-reply covers you.

## Messaging hygiene

- **Minimum necessary.** "Your lab results are ready in the portal" beats pasting the results into a text.
- **Verify identity** before discussing PHI with a new number, a caregiver, or a family member. Check for a HIPAA release on file.
- **Document** clinically meaningful exchanges in the chart. Most BAA platforms can export or copy a conversation.
- **Don't diagnose over text** for anything that needs an exam. Say so kindly and schedule a visit.
- **Photos from patients:** receive them only in the BAA platform, and move them to the chart with the EHR's media tools. Never save them to the device gallery.
- **Group texts:** avoid them. Never add a patient to a group.
- **Social media:** don't accept friend or follow requests from patients on personal accounts. Keep a practice page if you want a public presence.

## Handling boundary problems

| Situation | Response |
|---|---|
| Frequent after-hours non-urgent messages | Kindly restate the policy with a canned response. Offer a visit. Document it. |
| Messages that should be emergencies | Reply with emergency instructions immediately. Document. Consider a call. |
| Patient found your personal accounts or number | See [09 – Incident response](09-incident-response.md#patient-found-personal-number-or-accounts). |
| Harassment, threats, or stalking | Stop direct messaging. Loop in practice management, security, and risk management. Preserve records. See [09](09-incident-response.md). |
| Discharging a patient | Follow your practice's formal discharge process. Stop using the direct line with that patient and route them through the front desk. |
